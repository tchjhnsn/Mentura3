# Discovery interviews (Mentura)

Draft for Torian to edit. It builds on Naive's problem-interview template, which already follows *The Mom Test*. This is the no-stakes conversation. It never decides admission, and it never pitches Naive or the puzzle.

## What these interviews must learn

The three things we most need to know, from the market research of 2026-10-31:

1. **H2:** of founders who bootstrap Discovery and Validation, how many chose it and how many fell back on it because they could not raise? No source measures this, so it comes first.
2. **H1:** do founders seek outside money (VC, loans, accelerators) before they have validated?
3. **What they already pay** to get through Discovery and Validation: money, equity, time. This is the price anchor, not a stated price.

## Who to interview

- Fetch the ICP records in code before recruiting (rule of 07:15Z), and recruit against the primary ICP, `ai-native-early-founders`.
- Mix three groups, so the answers are not all from people who like you: founders from your own network; founders found through Capital Factory, Antler and Austin communities; founders who owe you nothing (cold outreach).
- Include founders who are trying to raise, founders who bootstrap, and founders who stopped. Only interviewing survivors biases the answers.
- Plan at least eight (the first eight by hand with a recorder, as already planned) and keep going until the answers repeat.
- Ask one question of Capital Factory or Antler staff separately: would they pay for founders who arrive validated? That settles whether founder mentors are customers or a channel.

## Outreach

One short message, no pitch. For example: "I'm talking to founders in Austin about how they got their venture from idea to first customers, especially how they paid for it. Would you give me 25 minutes? I'm not selling anything." Record who was asked, through which channel, and who said yes, so the response rate is evidence too.

## Ask (past facts, not opinions)

Start with the template's five questions, about the last time the problem happened. Then:

1. How did you pay for getting from idea to your first paying customer? Walk me through it.
2. Did you try to raise money, or get into an accelerator, before you had customers? What happened?
3. If you paid for it yourself: was that the plan from the start, or what you did when the other route didn't work?
4. What have you given up to get help, in money, equity or time? Who did you get it from? Was it worth it?
5. Who do you go to when you're stuck on a decision? How often?
6. What decision do you most wish you'd made differently in the first year? What did it cost?

## Do not

- Pitch Naive, Mentura or the puzzle, or show them anything. That belongs to the admission trial.
- Ask "would you pay for...?" or "how much would this be worth?" Stated prices run high; past spending does not.
- Mention the waitlist as a reward for the interview.

## After

- Notes and the recording go in `raw/`; cite them, never copy them into records.
- File each finding as an evidence item, as the template says: behavior, stated, commitment, and evidence against with `supports: false`.
- Tag each answer to H1, H2 or the price anchor, so the hypotheses get demonstrated or contradicted, not just discussed.
- Ask for one introduction to another founder. A given introduction is a commitment.
