# The industry as one venture: where Naive innovates

Oct 30, 2026 · @Torian

## Summary

Treated as one venture, the industry already does almost everything a solo founder needs done: it builds the product, runs departments with agents, teaches, scores ideas, stores documents and funds the best teams. What it does not do is keep the venture's own judgment: why it is the way it is, which decision matters now, and what evidence each commitment rests on. Every incumbent stores outputs (code, copy, pages, CRM rows, scores). None stores the venture's decisions as owned, checkable data that any tool or agent can read.

That gap grows as AI makes building nearly free. Agents now act fast on whatever premise the founder hands them, so the scarce thing moves from production to judgment. My hypothesis, not yet Torian's: **Naive innovates on the whole industry by being the venture's judgment layer**, a portable, evidence-graded venture record with a phase-aware decision docket and premise attack that every other tool can read and write.

For the MVP this means: engineer only what no one in the industry has (four items in the innovation section) and point at incumbents for everything else. The ICP evidence here is desk research. It needs the planned founder interviews before anyone treats it as validated.

## Method

The industry is defined by the founder's job, not by a product category: *get from an idea to a working venture without wasting years*. Every product that serves part of that job becomes one department of an imaginary company, Industry Inc.

1. **Bound the industry** by the job and list its segments (advice, validation, planning, building, running, workspace, legal and money).
2. **Write Industry Inc.'s Product Anatomy card**, the same card our own products get (job, core benefit, generic, expected, augmented, potential).
3. **Run Industry Inc., Naive and Mentura through one matrix** of factors. Industry Inc. scores the best any incumbent offers on each factor, not the average.
4. **Draw the strategy canvas and ERRC grid** (Kim and Mauborgne, *Blue Ocean Strategy*, 2005): what Naive eliminates, reduces, raises and creates.
5. **First-principles pass** (the Musk approach Torian named): strip the industry's conventions and ask what the job physically requires. Thiel's zero-to-one test is the check: is it new, or only better?

Scoring against the industry's best, rather than its average, is harsher than standard Blue Ocean. Something counts as innovation only if no one in the industry has it.

## The industry and the overlapping ICPs

Naive's ICP is solo founders and small founding teams in Discovery and Validation, building AI-native. That group is growing: solo-founded companies rose from 23.7% of new US startups in 2019 to 36.3% in the first half of 2025, yet solo-led companies took only 14.7% of priced-round cash in 2024 ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/solo-founders-report)).

Four overlapping ICP groups use the industry today: technical solo builders (Claude Code, Cursor, indie hackers), non-technical founders who vibe-code (Lovable, Bolt), first-time founders in programs (Startup School, accelerators), and side-project founders. The "how they use it" column is inferred from reviews and roundups, not observed.

## Where Naive innovates, and what that means for the MVP

From first principles, a venture is a sequence of commitments made under uncertainty: explore, prototype, charge, raise. Each should be proportional to the evidence behind it. AI has made production nearly free, so the industry's convention of spending on production is now spending on the cheap part. The scarce input is judgment about which commitment comes next, and nobody keeps it as data. That is the zero-to-one claim to test.

Four candidates, ranked by how new they are to the whole industry:

1. **Venture CI.** The venture's records are checked like code, and a commitment (charge, raise, hire) fails its check until the evidence ladder supports it. No incumbent has anything like it.
2. **The decision docket.** One command shows the decision that matters now for this phase, computed from the records, each with its premise attack attached. This answers Torian's own problem statement: no framework for which decisions matter now.
3. **The portable venture record.** A `package.json` for a venture that Claude Code, Lovable, Cofounder or Notion can read for context and write back to. Naive becomes the layer under the industry rather than another tool in it.
4. **Provenance and owner confirmation.** Every claim shows who made it, human or agent, and agent-drafted claims count as unconfirmed until the founder confirms them.

Proposed MVP, replacing the withdrawn cut: one founder, daily, can create a venture, keep identity, problem, evidence and decision records, run a new `naive next` for the docket, and run `naive check` (it exists today) extended to gate commitments. The record exports as context for any agent. Out of the MVP: the console, connectors, agents that do work, meeting intelligence, and templates beyond Discovery and Validation. Those either exist in the industry already or depend on the four above.

**Decided 2026-10-30 by Torian:** the MVP is anchored on the judgment layer. The founder interviews still test it.

Docs for each MVP piece follow README docs-as-code (2026-10-31): a plain `.md` beside the code it describes, updated in the same PR, linked to its neighbours, and shown behind the waitlist, not on the public site.

## What this does not know yet

The ranking above is my hypothesis from desk research, built in one pass on 2026-10-30. Before it decides engineering, these gaps need closing:

- **No ICP has been asked.** Every "how they use it" cell is inferred. The 8 hand-run interviews in the validation plan (PR #106) should test the four candidates directly: would a founder run a check before charging, and do they want a docket?
- **Most sources are search summaries, not opened pages.** Only Cofounder's home page was fetched. Pricing and customer counts are vendor claims.
- **Cofounder's memory and export are unknown.** If it already exports a structured venture record, candidate 3 weakens. A hands-on trial of Cofounder, Siift and macro would settle it.
- **Siift is the closest on judgment.** Its scored assumptions overlap candidate 2. The difference claimed here (records the founder owns, checks that gate commitments) is untested.
- **The matrix is not a record yet.** An industry or competitor record type in Naive (a gap found on 2026-10-28) would let this matrix be re-run, and the gap-hunt skill could call it.
