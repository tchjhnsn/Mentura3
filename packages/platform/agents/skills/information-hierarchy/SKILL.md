# Information hierarchy and best practice at each stage

## Writing

- Lead with the answer or the decision, then the few reasons that support it, then the detail (the pyramid principle,
  Minto). A reader who stops after the first sentence still has the point.
- One idea per paragraph, one level of the hierarchy per heading. Group siblings that are alike; do not mix a reason
  with an example at the same level.
- Name things in the reader's words. Internal names belong in records, not on public pages.

## Pages and sitemaps

- A sitemap is a hierarchy of what a visitor needs to decide, in the order they need it: what this is, who it is for,
  what it does for them, why believe it, what to do next.
- Each page has one job and one primary action. Plan empty, loading and error states with the page, not after it.

## Work in stages

- Work top down: the goal, then the process groups that serve it, then the activities, then the tasks. Do not start a
  lower level before the level above it says what it needs.
- At each stage, use the established practice for that stage and name it (for example: problem interviews before a
  solution in Discovery; a fixed sampling frame before market research). If you skip a stage, say so, so it can be
  looped back to.
